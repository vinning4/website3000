// The Architecture

Coded manually. No templates.

Hand-coded means exactly that — every line written deliberately, nothing generated by a template engine or assembled from third-party plugins. The result is pure HTML and CSS files served directly to the browser: no database queries, no server-side processing, nothing running in the background that can slow down, break, or be hacked.

For a business owner, this translates simply: a faster site, more secure, and one that's superior to the competition.

// The Details

Frequently asked questions.

How does website subscription work?

Think Netflix, but with a web developer on retainer. One fixed monthly fee, cancel anytime.

Unlike traditional web design, which usually means a large upfront fee and no support afterwards, the subscription spreads the cost and includes everything: build, launch, hosting, edits, security, performance monitoring, and contact form messages sent straight to your inbox. One direct line to your developer, zero technical headaches.

What is the minimum subscription length?

None. It's month to month from day one with no lock-in. Search visibility builds gradually, so staying a while helps, but that's entirely your call.

Do I need to manage anything technical?

Nothing at all. I handle the technical side and explain things in plain English whenever you ask. You'll always know what's happening, and I'm reachable for any questions.

Why don't you use WordPress, Squarespace, or Wix?

Those platforms are built for everyone, so they're optimised for no one. They rely on off-the-shelf templates and add-on tools that pile up, need constant updating, and slow a site down over time. WordPress, in particular, is a frequent target for hackers.

I code every site by hand, with only what it needs. There's nothing extra to slow it down, break, or be hacked. The result is a faster, leaner site, engineered for results. For a small business, this is the ideal architecture.

What's this about Google PSI scores?

PSI = PageSpeed Insights. It's a Google tool that scores website performance on mobile and desktop. Page speed affects user experience and is a confirmed search ranking signal, since visitors quickly abandon slow sites. Scores above 90 are considered strong; my sites score 98–100.

What happens if I cancel the subscription?

You can cancel anytime. This stops the hosting and care that keep the site running, so the site comes down. Your domain stays yours, and I'll help you move back to your previous site if you had one. If you'd rather keep the site, you can buy it outright: you get the code files and can host it wherever you like.

How does the subscription compare to paying upfront?

The one-time option ($3,000) gives you the finished site and full source code. After that, edits and maintenance are yours to handle, or you can pay a developer, usually by the hour. The subscription spreads the cost and includes an on-call developer actively managing the site. Over time your total spend is similar — the difference is who looks after it.

How can the subscription pay for itself?

Simple arithmetic. If your website generates just one new customer each month worth $150 or more, the subscription effectively covers itself. For many service businesses such as trades, catering or allied health, a single new customer is often worth several hundred dollars, which means the website doesn't need high traffic to generate positive returns. If your current website isn't generating these results, it may be worth a chat.

How does SEO work?

Both pricing options include on-page Search Engine Optimisation. In plain terms, I set up your site so Google can read it clearly: logical page hierarchy, clear titles and descriptions, fast loading, mobile-friendly layouts, lightweight image formats, clean code, and your business details so Google understands what you do and where. I can't promise rankings, and no one honestly can. SEO isn't instant — but a fast, well-structured site gives you the best foundation. This article has more detail: What is SEO?
